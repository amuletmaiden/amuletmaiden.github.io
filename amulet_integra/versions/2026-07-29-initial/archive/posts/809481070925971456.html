And in that never quiet feast-hall, wrought of wood-iron beneath faintsun skies, the Beast-Queen rose from her glutting and strode past her sycophants and concubines, each terrible in its own right, to approach the challenger. The Beast-Queen knew many shapes (near all of them large and powerful), much food (she ate whatever she chose to be food), and had lived centuries. She had struck down and chosen as food many challengers before this, a count she did not choose to remember but one of her concubine-accountants’ ledgers did. This latest in that line looked unexceptional, weaker than many, smaller than most. That the Beast-Queen designed to approach such a petty challenger rather than force it to attempt to pass through the length and fury of her pride spoke that she must have been feeling exceptional grace or bloodlust on that faintsun day.

When the two met, the challenger struck trifold, three blows as one blow. This was generally held as against causality, or at least insulting to it, but the challenger had been careful in planning. Knowing that it would not be able to strike a second blow, the challenger had long hoarded the special wealth and hidden connections that one needed to make the requisite bribes.

As the three blows are one blow, there was no order to them, but they are ordered thus:

  1. The first blow the challenger made to the Beast-Queen’s shape-changing organ, unweaving the fabric of it, so as to prevent its use.
  2. The second blow the challenger made to the Beast-Queen’s mouth, sintering lower jaw to upper, and preventing her from choosing the challenger as food.
  3. The third blow was simple and straightforward and spilled out the Beast-Queen’s life, marking the end of centuries on the hall floor.

The Beast-Queen, being very terrible, was not one to be struck alone. She gave a great blow to the challenger from her much chipped and resharpened claws. The blows were so quick that none were later able to say which of the two had struck first. The claws sundered half of the challenger from itself, revealing in the remainder a flesh hollow showing that the challenger had learned the secret of secreting one’s shape-changing organ outside the self. With its organ safe and hidden elsewhere, the challenger simply changed to a shape that was largely uninjured while the challenged spilled out on the hall floor.

The new Beast-Queen, terrible in aspect if yet small in stature, walked to the other end of her feast hall, through her pride, and began her glutting.
