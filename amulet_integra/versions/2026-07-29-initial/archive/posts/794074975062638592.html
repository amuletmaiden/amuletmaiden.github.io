A long blare from a little silver box atop the door. A gentler place might have a bell, or only the shuttering of the metal door to mark entry. Lady Amaryllis perceives the malice in this place.

“Hi, welcome to McDonald’s, what can I get for you?”

“Yes,” she acknowledges. A long pause, frustrated. “May I please have a deluxe quarter pounder with cheese?”

“Absolutely, do you want the meal or just the sandwich?”

“- permit me a moment’s conference.”

Lady Amaryllis lifts me from where I hang upon her neck. A glint of day catches upon my surface —

The reds and yellows of this terrain fade to soft mush beneath blinding emerald brilliance. The shapes of the world are molten plastic runoff in the soundless instants after a nuclear flash.

Nothing can be seen save the wishing jewel’s verdant light, neither sun nor storefront nor the backs of eyelids. Nothing can be heard save my warm clear voice, searingly sweet as it floods the caves of her ears:

“Just get the toy.”

Her hand closes around me, as if only the cup of her fingers could smother my light, as if only closing one’s eyes could turn them blind — but it does.

“Just the toy, please.”
